Road-freight operators schedule against route-planner estimates, and the OSRM estimate recorded in Delhivery's public trip data runs short almost every time: \fzLegsTotal{} legs, the median taking \fzMedianGapRatioDpOne{} times the estimate. We ask where this error concentrates and how confidently that can be said. A Welch audit with false-discovery control flags \fzBottlenecks{} corridors as slower than the network, but legs of one recurring service are correlated (design effect about \fzDepDesignEffectMedianDpOne{}), and on placebo data with meaningless corridor labels the same procedure returns about \fzAuditPlaceboDiscoveriesDpOne{} discoveries; a cluster-aware permutation test confirms \fzAuditRobustHeadlineSlower{} corridors at its pre-registered strictness. Corridor effects nonetheless persist between halves of the period (rank correlation \fzStabilitySpearmanDpTwo{}), and empirical-Bayes ranking is only slightly more stable across support floors than raw ranking. For prediction, a gradient-boosted residual over a hierarchical back-off baseline, whose cold-start fallback is fair, reaches \fzReportedMaeDpTwo{} minutes MAE against the baseline's \fzBarBackoffMaeDpTwo{} (difference \fzReportedVsBarDeltaDpTwo{}, day-block 95\% interval [\fzReportedVsBarCiLowDpTwo{}, \fzReportedVsBarCiHighDpTwo{}]), with the gain on corridors without history. We report two evaluation pitfalls, an optimiser default that disabled the chosen loss and a replay that inflated an alert precision to 72.1\% that is \fzExcPrecisionAsOfRatePct{}\% once history is joined as of each event. Code, pre-registrations and every result file are public.
