Road-freight operators schedule against route-planner estimates, and the OSRM estimate recorded in Delhivery's public trip data runs short almost every time: 26,369 legs, the median taking 2.0 times the estimate. We ask where this error concentrates and how confidently that can be said. A Welch audit with false-discovery control flags 273 corridors as slower than the network, but legs of one recurring service are correlated (design effect about 2.0), and on placebo data with meaningless corridor labels the same procedure returns about 680.2 discoveries; a cluster-aware permutation test confirms 0 corridors at its pre-registered strictness. Corridor effects nonetheless persist between halves of the period (rank correlation 0.91), and empirical-Bayes ranking is only slightly more stable across support floors than raw ranking. For prediction, a gradient-boosted residual over a hierarchical back-off baseline, whose cold-start fallback is fair, reaches 29.66 minutes MAE against the baseline's 32.39 (difference −2.74, day-block 95% interval [−3.41, −2.11]), with the gain on corridors without history. We report two evaluation pitfalls, an optimiser default that disabled the chosen loss and a replay that reported an alert precision of 72.1% where the as-of value is 58.6%. The data are one operator over 22 days, a limit we state plainly. Code, pre-registrations and every result file are public.
